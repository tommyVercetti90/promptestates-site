Is Dubai Hills Estate a good investment?

Dubai Hills Estate is often called a "safe" place to buy in Dubai. We look at DLD figures for the 12 months to 8 October 2026: what the district offers an investor, where the risks are and who it suits best.

In short
  • Gross apartment yields are 5.2–6.9%, higher than in Dubai Marina and Business Bay.
  • Prices barely rose in a year: +0.7%, with a median of about AED 25,800 per m² (AED 2,400 per sq ft).
  • The district has 25 projects under construction; the large phases hand over in 2029.
  • Monthly sales fell from 445 in January to about 170 a month since June 2026.
  • 40% of purchases use a mortgage: many buyers live in their homes.

What the district offers an investor

Above all, steady rental demand and liquidity. Dubai Hills Estate has its infrastructure in place: the park, the golf course, schools, clinics and Dubai Hills Mall. Families rent here for the long term, so vacancies are shorter and resale is easier: the district recorded 3,068 sales worth AED 19.3 billion in 12 months.

Yield versus price growth

Gross yield is about 6.9% for studios and 5.2–5.5% for one- to three-bedroom apartments — above Dubai Marina and Business Bay (4.1–4.7% for one and two bedrooms). But price growth over the year is close to zero: +0.7%. Right now the district is about rental income and preserving capital rather than fast appreciation.

Off-plan supply

The DLD registry lists 25 projects under construction in the district. Emaar's largest new phases — Palace Residences Hillside, Rosehill, Vida Residences Hillside and Parkwood — are due in 2029. When they complete, many units will reach the rental and resale market at once, which may hold back rents and prices in 2029–2030.

Market activity

Sales are slowing: 445 in January 2026 and about 170 a month since June. The median price meanwhile holds at AED 24,000–25,700 per m² (AED 2,230–2,390 per sq ft). Fewer sales at stable prices means the market is cooling, not falling — and it gives buyers more room to negotiate.

Who Dubai Hills suits

Buyers who will live in the home and families who value the setting. Investors who want steady rent and liquidity rather than the highest yield. Anyone planning to hold for 5+ years. If the goal is the most rental income per dirham invested, districts such as JVC yield more (5.7–6.8%) at about AED 16,100 per m² (AED 1,500 per sq ft).

What to check before buying

Compare the project's price with the district median and with actual sales in the project over 6 months. Check the developer and the planned handover date in the DLD registry. Work out the net yield after the service charge. For off-plan, make sure payments go into the project's escrow account.

Frequently asked questions

Are prices rising in Dubai Hills Estate?

Over the 12 months to 8 October 2026 the median price barely changed: +0.7%.

What yield do Dubai Hills apartments give?

Gross yield is 5.2–6.9% depending on unit type, higher than in Dubai Marina and Business Bay.

When do the new Dubai Hills projects hand over?

Emaar's large new phases — Palace Residences Hillside, Rosehill, Vida Residences Hillside and Parkwood — are due in 2029 in the DLD registry.
