Dubai: the emirate's history and where to buy property

In half a century a pearl-diving settlement on Dubai Creek became one of the world's main business and tourism hubs — and the region's busiest off-plan property market.

In short
  • In 1833 families of the Bani Yas tribe led by Maktoum bin Butti settled on Dubai Creek.
  • Oil was found in 1966, but Dubai bet on trade, logistics and tourism.
  • Foreigners were allowed to buy freehold homes in 2002.
  • The UAE's main off-plan market, with thousands of projects in the DLD registry.

History

In 1833 several hundred members of the Al Bu Falasah (Bani Yas tribe), led by Maktoum bin Butti, moved to Dubai Creek. That was the start of the Al Maktoum dynasty that still rules today. The town lived on pearling and trade. In the 1950s and 60s Sheikh Rashid dredged the Creek for larger ships, and after oil was found in 1966 he invested the revenue in the port, airport and infrastructure. On 2 December 1971 Dubai became a founding member of the UAE.

Dubai today

Jebel Ali port, the Emirates hub, free zones, the DIFC financial centre, tourism and real estate — oil is only a small part of the economy. The population has passed 4 million, most of them expatriates.

Where to buy

For waterfront living and rentals — Dubai Marina, Palm Jumeirah, Dubai Islands and Dubai Creek Harbour. The business core — Downtown and Business Bay. Family areas with a lower entry price — JVC, Dubai Hills, Arjan. For long-term growth near the new Al Maktoum airport — Dubai South.

Rules for foreign buyers

Since 2002 foreigners can buy freehold property in designated zones, and the 2006 property registration law put this on a legal footing. Buyers pay a 4% Dubai Land Department fee. A purchase of AED 2 million or more qualifies for the Golden Visa. For the 2-year investor visa there has been no minimum value since April 2026: a sole owner qualifies with a ready home at any price (previously AED 750,000).
