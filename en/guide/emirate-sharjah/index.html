Sharjah: the UAE's cultural capital and where to buy

Dubai's neighbour offers more affordable homes, museums and more traditional rules. Many people live in Sharjah and work in Dubai.

In short
  • The emirate has coastlines on both the Arabian Gulf and the Gulf of Oman (Khor Fakkan, Kalba).
  • In 1932 one of the region's first airports opened here — the Al Mahatta fort.
  • UNESCO named Sharjah the Cultural Capital of the Arab World in 1998.
  • Home prices are noticeably lower than in neighbouring Dubai.

History

Sharjah is ruled by the Al Qasimi dynasty, a major maritime power on the Gulf coast in the 18th and 19th centuries. In 1932 the Al Mahatta airfield opened as a stop for British airlines on the route to India — one of the first airports in the region. In 1971 the emirate became a founding member of the UAE.

Sharjah today

A centre of culture and education: dozens of museums, University City and an international book fair. The emirate keeps strict rules — alcohol is banned, for example. Industry, ports and free zones provide jobs, and proximity to Dubai makes Sharjah popular with families.

Where to buy

New master communities — Aljada (Arada) and Tilal City; waterfronts — Maryam Island, Al Mamsha and Sharjah Waterfront City. Payment plans with low down payments and prices below Dubai's are common here.

Rules for foreign buyers

Historically foreigners bought in Sharjah on long-term usufruct rights. In recent years the emirate has allowed full ownership in specific approved projects — check the type of title for a given project before buying. Transactions are registered with the Sharjah Real Estate Registration Department.
